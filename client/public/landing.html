Every time you read a line, your eyes do not glide smoothly across it. They move in short, sharp jumps called saccades, pausing four or five times to grab a small cluster of words before leaping ahead. The pauses, not the jumps, are where reading actually happens — your brain stitching the snapshots into meaning faster than you notice.

The average adult reads somewhere between two hundred and two hundred and fifty words per minute. Skilled readers push past three hundred while still following the argument, and trained speed readers can clear five hundred. But raw pace is only half the story. A reader who races through a page and remembers nothing has not really read it at all, which is why comprehension is measured alongside speed.

The encouraging part is that reading speed is a trainable skill. Widening the span of words your eyes take in per pause, resisting the habit of silently sounding out each word, and simply reading more all nudge the number upward. Like any sport, it rewards practice, measurement, and a little friendly competition.
